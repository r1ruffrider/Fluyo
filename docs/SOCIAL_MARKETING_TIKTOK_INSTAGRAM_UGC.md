# Fluyo TikTok, Instagram, and UGC growth plan

**Owner:** Fernanda, social growth
**Prepared:** October 17, 2026
**Status:** Draft for execution; no accounts, posts, ads, creator contracts, or spending are authorized by this document.

## 1. Objective and guardrails

Build an audience of English-speaking adults who want to use Spanish in real conversations, learn which motivations and creative angles generate qualified interest, and create a repeatable path from organic content to early access, account creation, and paid conversion.

Fluyo is currently a pre-launch product. The implemented application supports authentication, a basic profile, server-controlled Plus pricing, Stripe test-mode Checkout, subscription status, and billing management. Lessons, Charla, the live interpreter, camera/video translation, offline packs, native mobile apps, tutors, and learning progress are roadmap features. Social content must label those capabilities as **in development**, **planned**, **prototype**, or **concept** until the corresponding experience is usable and verified.

Allowed statements today:

- “Fluyo is building Spanish practice for real conversations.”
- “Follow the build,” “join early access” only after a real public signup destination exists, and “help shape the product.”
- Fluyo Plus is currently configured at **$12.99 monthly** or **$95.88 annually ($7.99/month equivalent)**. Mention prices only with “planned/current web pricing; subject to change before launch” until public production billing is authorized.
- The product vision includes two-way English/Spanish practice and translation. Present demonstrations as prototypes unless captured from a verified working feature.

Prohibited statements until evidence exists:

- “Learn Spanish in X days,” “become fluent,” guaranteed outcomes, invented reviews, user counts, app ratings, or conversion claims.
- “Available on the App Store/Google Play,” “download now,” or screenshots that imply a released mobile app.
- “Unlimited lessons,” “live translation,” “AI tutor,” or similar feature availability when the feature is not usable in the current build.
- Fabricated learner testimonials or a creator script presented as an independent opinion.

## 2. Audience and channel roles

### Primary audience

English-speaking adults aged roughly 25–55 with a concrete reason to speak Spanish:

1. **Travelers:** a trip, relocation, or recurring travel to Spanish-speaking destinations.
2. **Relationship learners:** a partner, in-laws, friends, or heritage connection.
3. **Workplace learners:** healthcare, education, hospitality, sales, service, and community roles.
4. **Restarting learners:** people who recognize vocabulary but freeze when speaking.

### TikTok role

Use fast, useful, personality-led videos for discovery and creative testing. Prioritize native vertical video, immediate spoken hooks, comments as research, duets/stitches only when rights and context permit, and Spark Ads only after an organic post proves engagement quality.

### Instagram role

Use Reels for reach, carousels for saved learning material, Stories for daily interaction, Highlights for persistent proof, and DMs for community research. Reels can reuse the same clean master as TikTok, but captions, covers, safe zones, and CTAs should be native to Instagram.

## 6. Publishing system: three posts per day per platform

Cadence is a 30-day launch experiment, not a permanent promise. The daily pattern gives each post a job:

- **Morning — utility (S/C):** a saveable lesson or speaking prompt.
- **Midday — discovery (R/J):** a relatable scene, story, or reaction.
- **Evening — participation/proof (B/P):** a poll, build update, product proof, or community response.

Recommended starting windows use the primary audience’s local time: 8:00, 13:00, and 19:00. After seven days, move each slot based on follower activity and qualified outcomes. Do not declare those times universally optimal.

### Reuse rules

- Record one clean 9:16, 1080×1920 master with no platform watermark, burned-in captions, and key text inside safe margins.
- TikTok version: faster first frame, spoken hook in the first second, looser caption, comment CTA, native licensed audio.
- Instagram Reel: designed cover, slightly more explanatory caption, save/share CTA, and Story reshare with a poll or question sticker.
- Convert one strong lesson each day into an Instagram carousel only when it adds reference value; this can replace the morning Reel, not create a fourth required feed post.
- A reused concept still gets a platform-specific caption and CTA. Do not cross-post a watermarked download.
- Stories are community support and are not counted among the three daily feed posts.

## 7. Production workflow

### Weekly batch

1. **Monday — research:** review comments, saves, search terms, retention, product status, and cultural accuracy needs.
2. **Tuesday — scripts:** write 21 concepts; assign pillar, stage label, hook, visual, CTA, hashtag cluster, and destination.
3. **Wednesday — review:** native-speaker language review, product-owner claim review, and safety/rights review.
4. **Thursday — record:** batch talking-head and roleplay footage in 9:16; capture product only from a verified build using synthetic test data.
5. **Friday — edit:** captions, motion, sound licensing, covers, alt text, and platform variants. Export without watermarks.
6. **Daily — publish and engage:** final status check, publish, respond for 30 minutes, log insights, and collect questions.
7. **Sunday — learn:** compare cohorts by post ID, select iterations, archive weak ideas, and plan the next week.

### Asset and approval record

Every asset should store: post ID, script version, language reviewer, claim source, talent release, audio license, disclosure requirement, product build/commit if shown, edit master, TikTok export, Instagram export, captions, alt text, UTM, approval, publication URL, and performance snapshot.

### Reusable script templates

**Utility lesson (15–25 sec)**

1. Hook: “If you only learn one phrase for [scene], use this.”
2. Demonstrate at natural speed.
3. Break it into two chunks.
4. Repeat naturally with on-screen text.
5. CTA: “Save it, then say it without reading.”

**Relatable skit (15–30 sec)**

1. First-frame problem: “POV: they answer faster than your app lesson.”
2. Show the freeze.
3. Give one recovery phrase.
4. Replay the scene successfully.
5. CTA: “Comment the scene that makes you freeze.”

**Build update (20–40 sec)**

1. Label: “Fluyo build update — concept/prototype/live.”
2. State one user problem.
3. Show one decision or verified workflow.
4. Ask one narrow research question.
5. Never promise a date without an approved release commitment.

**Comment response (10–20 sec)**

1. Show the real comment with permission/anonymization.
2. Answer one point directly.
3. Demonstrate with one example.
4. Ask the commenter whether that resolves it.

## 8. Community operations and moderation

- Respond to useful comments within 24 hours; prioritize questions, corrections, and personal stories over emojis.
- Like and pin high-quality community examples after checking accuracy.
- When corrected on language or culture, verify with a qualified/native reviewer, thank the contributor, and post a visible correction.
- Do not diagnose learning disabilities, give immigration/legal/medical advice, or invite minors into private conversations.
- Move account, billing, privacy, or harassment reports to a documented support channel; never request passwords or payment data in DMs.
- Hide/delete spam, scams, slurs, doxxing, sexual harassment, and impersonation. Preserve evidence for threats before reporting/blocking.
- Use saved replies as drafts and personalize them. Do not automate argumentative or sensitive responses.
- Weekly community report: top 10 questions, top 5 objections, language corrections, product requests, safety incidents, and content opportunities.

## 9. Measurement and experiment design

### Organic scorecard

Measure by post and pillar:

- 2-second and 6-second hold, average watch time, completion rate, and rewatches.
- Saves and shares per reach for utility content.
- Meaningful comments per 1,000 views for research/community content.
- Profile visits and qualified link clicks per 1,000 views.
- Early-access completion or account creation by UTM after a real destination exists.
- Follower growth is context, not the sole success metric.

### Experiment rules

- Change one major variable at a time: hook, talent, scene, offer, or CTA.
- Run at least three creative executions before rejecting a pillar.
- Compare posts within the same format and audience stage; avoid treating one viral outlier as strategy.
- A winning concept must produce both attention and a downstream quality signal such as saves, qualified comments, profile visits, or conversions.
- Keep a “claim risk” column; content requiring an unavailable feature cannot win its way past accuracy rules.

Weekly decisions:

- **Scale:** top-quartile qualified outcome twice, positive comment quality, and no accuracy/brand issue.
- **Iterate:** strong hook retention but weak saves/clicks; revise lesson value or CTA.
- **Stop:** three executions below median on both attention and qualified outcomes, or any misleading/unsafe premise.

## 10. UGC advertising plan

UGC means creator-made advertising that feels native to the channel. It does not mean inventing a user or disguising a paid actor as an independent customer. Until creators have used a working Fluyo learning experience, briefs should use **founder/build diary**, **problem-aware**, **educational**, or **concept reaction** formats—not testimonials or before/after results.

### Creator sourcing

Build a roster across:

- English-speaking Spanish learners at beginner/intermediate levels.
- Native Spanish-speaking teachers or bilingual communicators.
- Travel creators focused on Mexico, Spain, and Latin America.
- Relationship/heritage storytellers who choose to discuss their own authentic experience.
- Workplace creators in relevant contexts, avoiding regulated advice.

Sources: inbound creator form, TikTok One/Creator Marketplace where available, Instagram Creator Marketplace where available, direct outreach from brand accounts, existing educators, and referrals. Score each creator on audience fit, on-camera clarity, comment quality, content consistency, disclosure compliance, brand safety, and rate/rights fit. Follower count is secondary.

### Planning compensation assumptions

These are budgeting assumptions to validate through quotes; they are not market facts or promises:

| Deliverable                             |            Planning range (USD) | Included assumption                                                  |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------: | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New creator, one edited 15–30 sec asset |                       $150–$400 | One concept, one hook, one revision, organic brand usage for 30 days |
| Proven niche creator, one edited asset  |                     $400–$1,200 | Strong portfolio, raw footage, three hooks, one revision             |
| Specialist/bilingual educator           |                    $600–$2,000+ | Accuracy review and expertise premium                                |
| Paid usage extension                    | +30–100% of base per 30–90 days | Depends on platforms, territory, and spend                           |
| Category exclusivity                    |                       +20–100%+ | Narrow duration/category only; negotiate separately                  |

Never buy positive sentiment. Pay for the work, disclosure, and licensed usage; the creator retains the right to state an honest opinion.

### Rights checklis

Contract fields must state deliverables, deadline, revision count, payment timing, channels, territory, duration, organic use, paid use, editing/cutdown rights, raw footage, whitelisting/partnership-ad or Spark authorization, music rights, likeness, exclusivity, disclosure, data handling, cancellation, and takedown process. Do not assume perpetual global paid rights. Obtain a release from every identifiable person.

Creators must use clear disclosures. TikTok requires its content disclosure setting for posts promoting a brand, including the creator’s own brand; paid collaborations should tag the brand partner and may grant ad authorization. Instagram requires the paid partnership label for paid, gifted, or affiliate branded content. FTC guidance calls for hard-to-miss disclosure with the endorsement itself; in video, put it in the video and preferably both visually and audibly. References: [TikTok commercial-content disclosure](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service), [Instagram branded content](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/616901995832907), and [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers).

### Creator brief template

- **Objective:** the one audience belief/action to change.
- **Audience:** motivation, current behavior, and scene.
- **Truth boundary:** exact working feature or “in development” label.
- **Deliverables:** clean 9:16 master, 1080×1920, 15–35 sec, burned-in captions, raw clips, three hooks, two CTAs, no copyrighted audio.
- **Must show/say:** product-status disclosure, key message, visible sponsorship disclosure, approved destination.
- **Must avoid:** fluency promises, fabricated results, fake UI, competitor disparagement, stereotypes, and unsupported claims.
- **Creative freedom:** creator’s own wording and setting after factual review.
- **Review:** factual/legal review may correct claims; it must not force a false opinion.

### Initial UGC concepts and shot lists

#### Concept A — “I freeze when they answer”

- Hooks: “My problem isn’t knowing Spanish words”; “The lesson ends right before the hard part”; “I can order—until they ask a question.”
- Shots: creator studies phrase → real-life-style reply arrives fast → freeze → uses one recovery phrase → explains why conversation practice matters → Fluyo build-stage end card.
- CTA: “Follow Fluyo as we build practice for the reply.”

#### Concept B — “Trip countdown”

- Hooks: “I have 30 days before Mexico”; “I stopped studying random vocabulary”; “These are the five conversations I actually need.”
- Shots: calendar → destination → five scene cards → one roleplay → honest progress journal → early-access CTA when live.
- Boundary: no promise of fluency in 30 days.

#### Concept C — “Partner/family dinner”

- Hooks: “I want to join the conversation, not wait for a translation”; “The hardest part is jumping in”; “One question helped me participate.”
- Shots: respectful personal motivation → one useful question → practice attempts → creator reflection → follow/build CTA.
- Boundary: must be the creator’s real story or explicitly labeled dramatization.

#### Concept D — “Teacher reacts to the prototype”

- Hooks: “I teach Spanish; here’s what this practice flow gets right”; “Correction timing can change confidence”; “I would change one thing.”
- Shots: paid-partnership disclosure → prototype label → screen reaction → one strength → one honest improvement → question to audience.
- Boundary: creator must be allowed to criticize; no “expert approved” blanket claim.

#### Concept E — “Product proof after feature release”

- Hooks: “I tried one complete Fluyo practice”; “Here’s the part that made me answer out loud”; “A language demo with no cutaways.”
- Shots: disclosure → authenticated start → complete real workflow → result/state → honest reaction → verified CTA.
- Gate: do not produce until the shown learning workflow is publicly usable and has passed QA.

### Paid creative test matrix

Start only after conversion measurement and destination QA pass.

| Dimension        | Variants                                                              |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audience problem | travel / relationship / workplace / restart freeze                    |
| Hook type        | confession / POV / question / contrarian learning insight             |
| Talent           | learner / bilingual creator / teacher / founder                       |
| Proof            | useful lesson / roleplay / verified product demo / build transparency |
| CTA              | follow / join early access / start free / view verified feature       |
| Length           | 12–15 sec / 20–25 sec / 30–40 sec                                     |

Phase 1: test 4 concepts × 3 hooks organically.
Phase 2: take the strongest six posts into small, equal-budget paid cells—TikTok Spark Ads and Instagram partnership ads where rights permit.
Phase 3: test CTA/destination while holding creative constant.
Phase 4: produce fresh creator variations of proven concepts before increasing spend.

Meta recommends native 9:16 Reels creative with audio in safe zones and supports promoting creator content through partnership ads; TikTok supports creator-post authorization through content disclosure/brand tagging and Spark Ads. See [Meta Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads) and [TikTok Content Suite/Spark Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-use-content-suite-to-create-and-launch-spark-ads?lang=en).

### Funnel and events

1. **Attention:** view and qualified hold.
2. **Engagement:** save, share, meaningful comment, profile visit.
3. **Intent:** click to a source-tagged public page.
4. **Lead/account:** confirmed early-access opt-in or verified account creation.
5. **Activation:** first verified learning action once that feature ships.
6. **Revenue:** server-confirmed subscription after authoritative billing synchronization, never a redirect alone.

Create platform events only for real user actions with consent. Do not optimize to a demo form success message or Checkout return URL.

### Paid KPIs and scale/stop rules

Use business economics after data exists; do not hard-code invented benchmarks. Track thumb-stop/hold, completion, outbound CTR, landing-page completion, cost per confirmed lead/account, activation rate, paid conversion, CAC, payback, refund/cancellation, and creative fatigue.

- **Scale 20–30% at a time** only when a cell has enough conversions to be directionally stable, its confirmed downstream cost is within the approved acquisition target, comments show audience fit, and no misleading-claim issue exists.
- **Iterate** when hook retention is strong but CTR is weak, or CTR is strong but the destination conversion is weak. Diagnose the appropriate layer.
- **Stop immediately** for rights/disclosure problems, material negative feedback about deception, invalid tracking, broken destination, wrong audience, or unsupported claims.
- **Stop a normal underperformer** after it spends the pre-approved test cap without a confirmed downstream result or performs materially worse than the control across two comparable runs.
- Refresh before frequency and performance decay become persistent; retain the concept and change creator/hook before discarding a proven audience problem.
